## 2. Calibration wizard and fitting editor (Settings > Calibration wizard & fitting editor…)
Four free-order tabs: **1 Weights** (profile, reference weights with nominal/assigned mass and unit, combination loads), **2 Observations**, **3 Fit**, **4 Review**. "Load the active calibration" imports its observations for refitting (the demo can be refitted).
- **Observations** store RAW counts (independent of tare and active calibration): record N seconds from the shown sensor (reuses the Measurement machinery via `beginMeasurement(target, hooks)`: highlighted, logged, cancelled by tare/gap), use the selected interval, or type a value. Each gets `analyzeSeries` validity; an `invalid` one starts excluded. Placement index and "removed and replaced" are stored.
- **Fit models:** piecewise-linear through group means (= v11 behaviour), straight line, quadratic, cubic. Polynomials are fitted as **counts on mass** (noise is in the counts; avoids regression dilution), equal weights, in a normalised variable. The engine is unchanged: the file contains anchors **sampled on the fitted curve** (segments doubled until the deviation from the exact inverse is < 1e-5 of the mass span, max 256; the value is shown and stored in `fit.maxInterpolationErrorMg`). Non-monotonic fits and too few masses are rejected. Fit block + all observations (excluded ones marked `excluded`) are written into the same `schemaVersion` 1 file. Reference masses with different assigned masses are not merged.
- Fit tab: model comparison (p, dof, residual SD, RMS, max), plots (counts vs mass with curve, residuals), replicate scatter per mass, residual table. Residuals are observed scatter, **not** uncertainty; `validation.status` stays `not-characterized`.
- Review: errors/warnings, Activate on the shown sensor (new epoch, tare reset), Export JSON. The wizard is bound to one sensor.

## 4. Design note: weighting of the fit (planned; v12 uses equal weights)
- **What v12 does.** Ordinary least squares: every included observation counts the same, i.e. it minimises SSE = sum (raw_i - g(m_i))^2. This is the best linear unbiased estimator only if the scatter of an observation around the curve has the **same variance at every mass** (homoscedastic) and the observations are independent.
- **Why this is a simplification.** Load-cell placement scatter often grows with load, the empty platform usually scatters least, and the number of placements per mass is rarely equal. With equal weights the noisy heavy loads (which also have the highest leverage on the slope) dominate; the estimate is still unbiased but not the most precise one, and the single residual SD s = sqrt(SSE/(n-p)) is an average over very different scatter levels.
- **Which variance matters.** Not the within-recording SD (`rawStdDev`): consecutive samples are autocorrelated and its mean-error is tiny after n_eff samples (demo: sample SD about 2.4 counts, mean error of a 2000-sample recording well below 1 count). What limits the observation is the **between-placement scatter** (demo: 4998 / 5000 / 5002 for three 100 g placements, SD 2 counts). Therefore sigma_k^2 per reference mass k = sample variance of the observation means of that mass, nu_k = n_k - 1 degrees of freedom.
- **Weighted LS.** Minimise sum w_i (raw_i - g(m_i))^2, w_i = 1 / sigma_i^2. Check: reduced chi-square sum w r^2 / (n-p) should be about 1; much larger means the model is wrong (or sigma too small), much smaller means sigma was overestimated.
- **Why it is not just "plug in the replicate variance".** With 3 placements, s_k^2 has only 2 degrees of freedom (a 95 % interval of roughly 0.27 to 39 times the true value); a lucky triple with s_k ~ 0 would receive an almost infinite weight. Planned estimators, in order of preference:
  1. **Shrinkage:** sigma~_k^2 = (nu_k s_k^2 + nu_0 s_p^2) / (nu_k + nu_0), s_p^2 = pooled variance of all replicate groups, nu_0 about 2-4 (documented constant).
  2. **Variance model:** sigma(m) = c0 + c1 m fitted to the group SDs (needs at least 3 masses with at least 2 placements), evaluated at every mass, also for masses with a single placement.
  3. **Fallback to equal weights**, announced in the UI, if there are fewer than 3 distinct masses or fewer than 2 replicates anywhere.
  A floor (e.g. the sensor's quantisation step divided by sqrt 12, or n_eff-based within-recording error) prevents zero variances.
- **Where it shows.** `fit.weighting = {scheme: "equal" | "shrinkage" | "variance-model", nu0, floor, sigmaPerMass:[...]}`, reduced chi-square in the fit table, weights column in the residual table, standardised residuals in the residual plot. The sampled-anchor output is unchanged (it only needs g).
- **Anchors model** already averages replicates (equal weight per placement); weighting only changes polynomial / line fits.

## 5. Design note: weight groups ("Meta calibration", working name)
Idea (from earlier sessions): several uncertified weights with the same claimed mass (e.g. 3 x 100 g) are used together; each weight is stored **independently** in the calibration file; the group average is used for the anchor.
- **What averaging buys.** It averages down the random part: placement scatter and the unknown deviations of the individual weights from nominal. If the three weight errors are independent with SD sigma_w, the error of their mean mass is about sigma_w / sqrt 3.
- **What it cannot buy.** A common bias (same manufacturer, same density or adjustment, all too heavy or light) does not average out. Without at least one certified mass, the scale of the calibration rests on the nominal values; the claimed accuracy can never be better than the weights' class tolerance. The file must say so (`massBasis: "nominal, uncertified"`), and the UI must not show an uncertainty that the weights cannot support.
- **What v12 does with such weights.** The three 100 g weights have equal effective mass and are therefore pooled into one anchor group, and the "SD between placements" of that group mixes placement scatter with weight-to-weight differences. A single placement per weight (as in the demo) cannot separate the two.
- **Planned: variance components.** With at least 2 placements per weight: one-way random-effects ANOVA per group. Within-weight (placement) variance s_w^2 from repeated placements of the same weight; between-weight variance s_b^2 = max(0, (MS_between - MS_within)/n_placements). Report both. If s_b is clearly larger than s_w, the weights are not equal in practice (a finding, not an error); then either keep them as separate masses with their own anchors or keep the group with the larger variance in the weighting.
- **Planned: relative masses of the weights.** The sensor compares the weights with much better precision than the nominal values are known: delta_ij = (r_i - r_j) / slope. Store these as `relativeMassToGroupMean` per weight (with the sensor-repeatability error). This makes the weights usable later as check weights and shows which one deviates.
- **Planned (advanced): combinations.** Loads such as A+B, A+B+C extend the range, but their masses share the weights' errors (correlated). A joint fit with the true weight masses as parameters and the nominal masses as priors (sigma_nom from the class tolerance) handles this; at least one trusted mass (certified, or an assumed group mean) must fix the scale, otherwise scale and mass errors are not separable. Out of scope until the simple version works.
- **Schema (additive, `schemaVersion` stays 1; unknown keys are already ignored by v12):** `referenceWeightGroups: [{groupId, name, nominalMass, unit, memberWeightIds, massBasis: "nominal" | "assigned", aggregation: "mean-of-placements" | "variance-components"}]`, `referenceWeights[].groupId`, observations keep `weightId` (individual weight) and `placementIndex`; the anchor lists `sourceObservationIds` of all members.
- **Name.** "Meta calibration" is ambiguous (in metrology "meta" suggests a calibration of calibrations). Candidates: **"Weight group"** (UI) / `referenceWeightGroups` (file). Decision to confirm.
- **Grouping rule to settle:** v12 groups by equal effective mass. Planned: group by an explicit `groupId`; weights with different assigned masses and no group stay separate masses.

## 6. Design note: Simple and Advanced wizard modes
One data model and one file format; the mode only changes what is shown and which defaults apply (so a file made in Simple mode opens in Advanced and vice versa).
- **Simple:** steps in order with a progress hint; weights entered by nominal mass only (e.g. "3 x 100 g, 1 x 200 g"); the wizard proposes the placement sequence and asks for 3 placements per load (remove and replace); fixed recording length with the validity check running and a plain-language verdict ("settled" / "still drifting"); model chosen by the wizard (anchors, or a straight line if the residuals are small and the masses are at least 3), equal or shrinkage weights, no polynomial degrees, no assigned masses, warnings in short plain sentences; activation with a reminder to check with a known weight.
- **Advanced / professional:** everything of v12 plus assigned (certificate) masses and uncertainties, weight groups with variance components, weighting choice, polynomial degrees, exclusion reasons, ascending/descending load order (hysteresis), environment fields (temperature, humidity, warm-up), leave-one-out residuals, and export of the full fit report.
- **Where the mode lives:** `wizardMode: "simple" | "advanced"` in `ui-settings.json` (browser copy / server default as for the other UI settings; schema addition, default "simple"). A switch inside the wizard header changes it at any time without losing data.
- **Decided:** "Weight group" is the name. A failed validity check never blocks: Simple mode offers **"include anyway"** with a warning, and the choice is recorded in the JSON (observation: `included: true`, `validityStatus`, `overrideReason: "included anyway by user"`, `overrideAt`; fit/review: list of overridden observations). Same rule, confirmed: a fit without degrees of freedom (no replicates) is allowed with a visible warning that is stored in the file (`fit.warnings`).
- **Open:** Qty column (section 8), reverse-order measurement (section 9), uncertainty (section 10).

## 8. Wizard UI: the "Qty" column (v12)
Qty only feeds the button "Add combination from Qty": it says how many pieces of that weight are placed on the platform together in one load (A=1, B=1 gives "A + B"; stored as `components[].quantity`). It does nothing else and is confusing. Planned: remove it. Each physical weight is its own entry (weight groups); a combination is made in a small dialog with one checkbox per weight.

## 9. Reverse-order measurement (hysteresis) - not in v12
v12 stores the placement order (`placementIndex`) but has no ascending/descending procedure: `reserved.loadingDirection` stays "not-determined" and `hysteresisAnalysis` is null. Planned (Advanced; optional "return run" in Simple): guided run up (empty, light ... heavy) then down; each observation gets `loadingDirection: "up" | "down"`; per mass the difference mean(down) - mean(up) is reported as hysteresis; the fit may use both directions together (default, the curve is then the mean path and the hysteresis is listed as a separate component) or one direction only.

## 10. Uncertainty (not in v12; planned in stages)
v12 states no uncertainty (`validation.status = "not-characterized"`); residual scatter is shown as observed scatter only. The `referenceUncertainty` field of a weight exists but has no input.
- **Stage 1 (cheap):** input per weight: `massUncertainty: {value, unit, basis: "certificate" | "class-tolerance" | "user-estimate" | "none", coverageFactor, distribution}`. Certificate: stated U and k, u = U/k. Only limits known (class tolerance, hobby weights "+-x mg"): rectangular distribution, u = limit / sqrt 3. Class table values (OIML R 111) must be checked against the standard before they are built in. UI: weights with basis other than "certificate" get a visible warning "not traceable"; JSON: `traceable: false`, `warnings`.
- **Stage 2:** uncertainty of the calibration at mass m: components = weights' mass uncertainty (a group mean of n independent weights: u/sqrt n; common bias is NOT reduced, which is stated), placement repeatability (s/sqrt n), fit residual scatter, reading noise (n_eff), resolution; combined as root-sum-of-squares; expanded U = k u_c (k = 2 only if the effective degrees of freedom allow it, otherwise a larger k from the t-distribution). Not included and listed as such: temperature, creep, hysteresis (until section 9), off-centre loading.
- **Stage 3:** a measurement in the Workbench combines this calibration uncertainty with its own repeatability and shows the result with a clearly labelled "estimated, not traceable" note when any input was uncertified.
- **File:** a separate block `uncertainty: {status: "estimated", traceable: false, components: [...], combined, k, basis}`; `validation.status` stays "not-characterized" (validated means checked against an independent reference).
